S001. BIOLOGY 240 19.2 HUMAN ENDOCRINE SYSTEM The endocrine glands and hormone producing diffused tissues/cells located in different parts of our body constitute the endocrine system
S002. Pituitary, pineal, thyroid, adrenal, pancreas, parathyroid, thymus and gonads (testis in males and ovary in females) are the organised endocrine bodies in our body (Figure 19.1)
S003. In addition to these, some other organs, e.g., gastrointestinal tract, liver, kidney, heart also produce hormones
S004. A brief account of the structure and functions of all major endocrine glands and hypothalamus of the human body is given in the following sections
S005. 19.2.1 The Hypothalamus As you know, the hypothalamus is the basal part of diencephalon, forebrain (Figure 19.1) and it regulates a wide spectrum of body functions
S006. It contains several groups of neurosecretory cells called nuclei which produce hormones
S007. These hormones regulate the synthesis and secretion of pituitary hormones
S008. However, the hormones produced by hypothalamus are of two types, the releasing hormones (which stimulate secretion of pituitary hormones) and the inhibiting hormones (which inhibit secretions of pituitary hormones)
S009. For example a hypothalamic hormone called Gonadotrophin releasing hormone (GnRH) stimulates the pituitary synthesis and release of gonadotrophins
S010. On the other hand, somatostatin from the hypothalamus inhibits the release of growth hormone from the pituitary
S011. These hormones originating in the hypothalamic neurons, pass through axons and are released from their nerve endings
S012. These hormones reach the pituitary gland through a portal circulatory system and regulate the functions of the anterior pituitary
S013. The posterior pituitary is under the direct neural regulation of the hypothalamus (Figure 19.2)
