S006. Hence, it is also called as anti- diuretic hormone (ADH)
S007. An impairment affecting synthesis or release of ADH results in a diminished ability of the kidney to conserve water leading to water loss and dehydration
S008. This condition is known as Diabetes Insipidus
S009. 19.2.3 The Pineal Gland The pineal gland is located on the dorsal side of forebrain
S010. Pineal secretes a hormone called melatonin
S011. Melatonin plays a very important role in the regulation of a 24-hour (diurnal) rhythm of our body
S012. For example, it helps in maintaining the normal rhythms of sleep-wake cycle, body temperature
S013. In addition, melatonin also influences metabolism, pigmentation, the menstrual cycle as well as our defense capability
S014. 19.2.4 Thyroid Gland The thyroid gland is composed of two lobes which are located on either side of the trachea (Figure 19.3 a)
S015. Both the lobes are interconnected with a thin flap of connective tissue called isthmus
S016. The thyroid gland is composed of follicles and stromal tissues
S017. Each thyroid follicle is composed of follicular cells, enclosing a cavity
S018. These follicular cells synthesise two hormones, tetraiodothyronine or thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3)
S019. Iodine is essential for the normal rate of hormone synthesis in the thyroid
S020. Deficiency of iodine in our diet results in hypothyroidism and enlargement of the thyroid gland, commonly called goitre
