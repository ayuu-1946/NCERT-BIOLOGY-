S001. 243 243 CHEMICAL COORDINATION AND INTEGRATION retardation, low intelligence quotient, abnormal skin, deaf-mutism, etc. In adult women, hypothyroidism may cause menstrual cycle to become irregular
S002. Due to cancer of the thyroid gland or due to development of nodules of the thyroid glands, the rate of synthesis and secretion of the thyroid hormones is increased to abnormal high levels leading to a condition called hyperthyroidism which adversely affects the body physiology
S003. Exopthalmic goitre is a form of hyperthyroidism, characterised by enlargement of the thyroid gland, protrusion of the eyeballs, increased basal metabolic rate, and weight loss, also called Graves’ disease
S004. Thyroid hormones play an important role in the regulation of the basal metabolic rate
S005. These hormones also support the process of red blood cell formation
S006. Thyroid hormones control the metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats
S007. Maintenance of water and electrolyte balance is also influenced by thyroid hormones
S008. Thyroid gland also secretes a protein hormone called thyrocalcitonin (TCT) which regulates the blood calcium levels
S009. 19.2.5 Parathyroid Gland In humans, four parathyroid glands are present on the back side of the thyroid gland, one pair each in the two lobes of the thyroid gland (Figure 19.3 b)
S010. The parathyroid glands secrete a peptide hormone called parathyroid hormone (PTH)
S011. The secretion of PTH is regulated by the circulating levels of calcium ions
S012. Parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases the Ca2+ levels in the blood
S013. PTH acts on bones and stimulates the process of bone resorption (dissolution/ demineralisation)
S014. PTH also stimulates reabsorption of Ca2+ by the renal tubules and increases Ca2+ absorption from the digested food
S015. It is, thus, clear that PTH is a hypercalcemic hormone, i.e., it increases the blood Ca2+ levels
S016. Along with TCT, it plays a significant role in calcium balance in the body
S017. 19.2.6 Thymus The thymus gland is a lobular structure located between lungs behind sternum on the ventral side of aorta
S018. The thymus plays a major role in the development of the immune system
S019. This gland secretes the peptide hormones called thymosins
S020. Thymosins play a major role in the differentiation of T-lymphocytes, which provide cell-mediated immunity
S021. In addition, thymosins also promote production of antibodies to provide humoral immunity
S022. Thymus is degenerated in old individuals resulting in a decreased production of thymosins
S023. As a result, the immune responses of old persons become weak
