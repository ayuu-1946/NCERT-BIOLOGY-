--- 27 SOURCE SENTENCES ---
S001. BIOLOGY 250 only one hormone, while pars nervosa (neurohypophysis) secretes two hormones
S002. The pituitary hormones regulate the growth and development of somatic tissues and activities of peripheral endocrine glands
S003. Pineal gland secretes melatonin, which plays a very important role in the regulation of 24-hour (diurnal) rhythms of our body (e.g., rhythms of sleep and state of being awake, body temperature, etc.)
S004. The thyroid gland hormones play an important role in the regulation of the basal metabolic rate, development and maturation of the central neural system, erythropoiesis, metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins and fats, menstrual cycle
S005. Another thyroid hormone, i.e., thyrocalcitonin regulates calcium levels in our blood by decreasing it
S006. The parathyroid glands secrete parathyroid hormone (PTH) which increases the blood Ca2+ levels and plays a major role in calcium homeostasis
S007. The thymus gland secretes thymosins which play a major role in the differentiation of T-lymphocytes, which provide cell-mediated immunity
S008. In addition, thymosins also increase the production of antibodies to provide humoral immunity
S009. The adrenal gland is composed of the centrally located adrenal medulla and the outer adrenal cortex
S010. The adrenal medulla secretes epinephrine and norepinephrine
S011. These hormones increase alertness, pupilary dilation, piloerection, sweating, heart beat, strength of heart contraction, rate of respiration, glycogenolysis, lipolysis, proteolysis
S012. The adrenal cortex secretes glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids
S013. Glucocorticoids stimulate gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, proteolysis, erythropoiesis, cardio-vascular system, blood pressure, and glomerular filtration rate and inhibit inflammatory reactions by suppressing the immune response
S014. Mineralocorticoids regulate water and electrolyte contents of the body
S015. The endocrine pancreas secretes glucagon and insulin
S016. Glucagon stimulates glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis resulting in hyperglycemia
S017. Insulin stimulates cellular glucose uptake and utilisation, and glycogenesis resulting in hypoglycemia
S018. Insulin deficiency and/or insulin resistance result in a disease called diabetes mellitus
S019. The testis secretes androgens, which stimulate the development, maturation and functions of the male accessory sex organs, appearance of the male secondary sex characters, spermatogenesis, male sexual behaviour, anabolic pathways and erythropoiesis
S020. The ovary secretes estrogen and progesterone
S021. Estrogen stimulates growth and development of female accessory sex organs and secondary sex characters
S022. Progesterone plays a major role in the maintenance of pregnancy as well as in mammary gland development and lactation
S023. The atrial wall of the heart produces atrial natriuretic factor which decreases the blood pressure
S024. Kidney produces erythropoietin which stimulates erythropoiesis
S025. The gastrointestinal tract secretes gastrin, secretin, cholecystokinin and gastric inhibitory peptide
S026. These hormones regulate the secretion of digestive juices and help in digestion
